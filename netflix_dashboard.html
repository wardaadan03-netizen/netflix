Executive summary

The catalogue is 70% movies and 30% TV shows, and the mix is shifting toward series: TV's share of releases rose from 16% (2008) to 46% (2020), while movie releases peaked in 2018 and have fallen 33% since. Three models were built on title metadata. They are useful for triage and discovery but not yet for investment decisions, because the data holds no viewing or engagement signal.

Key findings

  • Growth then plateau. Titles added per year peaked in 2019 at 2,014; 2021 is partial (to 25 Sep).
  • International content is the largest category. "International Movies" (2,752 titles) and "International TV Shows" (1,348) are the most common genre tags.
  • Few Kids titles. Kids content is 7% of rated movies and 17% of rated TV shows; Adults and Teens dominate.
  • Forecast. TV releases are projected near 650 by 2025 (range 449-851); movies stay flat near 517. The total is unreliable: 953 from its own model vs 1,168 when the parts are added.
  • Models. A Gradient Boosting predicts maturity group with 69% accuracy (macro-F1 0.72) vs a 46% baseline, weakest on Teens (58% recall). The recommender finds same-franchise titles in its top 10 for 53% of titles using metadata alone.

Approach

Raw data was cleaned (placeholders to nulls, near-duplicates removed, dates and durations parsed), explored, then modelled: a TF-IDF content recommender evaluated by franchise recovery, a rolling-origin backtested release-year forecast, and a maturity classifier with leakage controls and train-only feature selection. All numbers on this page are computed from those outputs.

Limitations

  • This is a catalogue snapshot, not viewership: nothing here shows what people watch or like.
  • 2021 is partial, and recent release years are probably under-counted because titles keep arriving after release.
  • Country labels are unreliable, so no country conclusions are drawn.
  • The forecast rests on 13 yearly points and a trend break; treat it as a scenario.
  • Movie vs TV Show classification was set aside: genre labels give the answer away and "no director" alone scores 95%.
